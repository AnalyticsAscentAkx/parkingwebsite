ANWB

ANWB Parkeren - no fee stated

ANWB says its app adds no extra fee, and a free ANWB Parkeren account gives discounts on some street parking. Street parking in the Netherlands only; it shows charge points but does not pay for charging.

Charging through a parking app

EasyPark applies its 15% service fee to charging sessions too, and its tariff page sets no cap for charging. A 30 kWh top-up at the national median of €0.41 per kWh costs €12.30; the fee adds €1.84, so the charge works out at about €0.47 per kWh. Before you charge through the app, compare that with the price your charge card pays at the same post on the charger map, which prices the parking and the charge together.

App setup tips

Register the number plate of your car correctly. Add a payment method in advance. Most apps let you set a maximum time so you do not pay for a forgotten session.

Which zones accept apps?

Paid street parking zones in the large cities accept the main apps. The sign or machine at the zone shows the zone code to enter; a wrong code is paying for a different street.

Blue zones: apps don't work

Blue parking disc zones (blauwe zone) are free for a limited time and do not use payment apps. You need a physical parking disc (parkeerschijf), sold at petrol stations and ANWB shops.

Paying at the charger

Most Dutch street posts still need a charge card

New fast chargers must accept a bank card, but the majority of the roughly 70,000 street posts do not: you start the session with a charge card or the operator's own app. One card that is accepted across the Dutch networks saves you installing an app per operator, and it is the difference between charging and walking away. Prices still vary by operator, so check the price per kWh on the map before you plug in; the national median is €0.37.
