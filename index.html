Charging in the Netherlands, in numbers

The national charge point register lists 78,630 public charging locations with 196,151 charge points, and this site reads their status every half hour. Right now 2.5% of locations report at least one point out of order. The median published price is €0.37 per kWh, but the spread between operators is wide, so the same 20 kWh can cost half or double depending on whose post you pick; the map shows the operator's price on every pin. Five operators run 71% of all posts: Vattenfall InCharge (26%), EQUANS (14%), TotalEnergies (12%), Allego (11%), Qwello Benelux BV (8%). 1,013 locations offer 150 kW or more.

The fleet is growing faster than the network: 2,023,337 cars with electric drive on 1 January 2026, up 24.8% in a year, against roughly 15.1 public charge points per 100 privately owned EVs nationally. In the big cities, where few people have a driveway, that figure is two to three times higher; in the wealthy commuter towns where EV ownership is highest, it is a fraction of it. The EV adoption map shows the pattern for all 342 municipalities.

What a stop costs is rarely just the electricity. Most street posts stand on paid parking spots, so a two-hour charge in a city centre adds a parking tariff that can exceed the charging itself. That is why every price on this site is the sum of both for the exact time you enter. New fast chargers must accept a bank card at the post under EU rules since 2024; older street posts still need a charge card or app, and the map says which is which.

Parking in the Netherlands - the complete picture

The Netherlands has one of the most organised - and most strictly enforced - parking systems in Europe. Whether you're a tourist driving in from Germany, an expat navigating Dutch city centres, or a local trying to avoid a €82.00 parking fine, knowing how parking works here saves real money.

How much does parking cost in the Netherlands?

Prices vary dramatically by city and zone. Amsterdam is the most expensive, with street parking in the centre costing €8.05 per hour - 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, with no Sunday discount. Rotterdam charges €4.24/hr, The Hague €3.98/hr, Utrecht €5.10/hr, and smaller cities like Delft and Zwolle from €1.40-2.90/hr. Garages are often cheaper than street parking - sometimes by 50% - and P+R (Park and Ride) locations can bring costs down to €1-5 per full day. For exact garage-level prices, our parking search computes the cost of your specific stay from the official national register tariff tables.

Free parking in the Netherlands

Free parking in the Netherlands is more common than most visitors expect. Outside Amsterdam, the majority of Dutch cities offer free parking on Sundays - including Rotterdam, The Hague, Eindhoven, Haarlem, Leiden, Delft, Maastricht, Groningen, Breda, Tilburg, and Zwolle. Most cities also have free parking from approximately 21:00 until the next morning on weekdays. Blue Zone areas - marked by blue road paint - allow 1-2 hours free with a parking disc (parkeerschijf), available for €2 at any petrol station. For longer stays, P+R locations offer subsidised daily parking from €1 in Amsterdam to €5 in Utrecht.

Street parking rules every driver must know

Dutch parking enforcement is automated and aggressive. Scan cars (scanauto's) photograph every plate on every street, every hour. A missed payment results in a €82.00 fine plus the unpaid fee - regardless of whether you're Dutch, German, British, or American. Foreign plates are fully tracked through European databases. Always pay via parking meters, the EasyPark app, or Yellowbrick - whichever covers your city. Both apps charge by the minute and let you stop the session remotely, saving €2-5 per visit compared to rounding up at a machine.

The smartest parking strategy in the Netherlands

For a full day in the centre, a P+R is usually the cheapest choice if you are happy to take a tram or train for the last stretch; for stops under two hours a nearby garage often wins once you count the transit ticket and the time. Park at a designated P+R facility on the outskirts, use public transport into the centre, and pay a fraction of what you'd spend on centre parking. Amsterdam's P+R costs just €1 for 24 hours. Rotterdam's is €2.50. The Hague's is €2. For airport visits, Schiphol's official P1 garage charges €54/day - but locals use the P+R Sloterdijk trick to get in and out for around €6 total. This site covers every option across 14 Dutch cities so you always know exactly what to pay - and what to avoid.
