What the map shows

Every municipality is coloured by the share of privately owned cars that run on electric drive on 1 January 2026, the CBS count from the national vehicle register. Switch the view to see public charge points per 100 electric cars, which is this site's own calculation from 78,584 charging locations placed inside the municipal boundaries. Tap a municipality for its figures; the Dutch charger pages for the larger towns are linked in the tables below.

Where electric ownership is highest, and lowest

The national share for private cars is 16.0%. The spread between municipalities is more than threefold: Rozendaal sits at 28.0%, Laren at 25.1% and Amstelveen at 24.8%, while Achtkarspelen closes the list at 8.2%, with Dantumadiel (8.4%) and Staphorst (8.8%) just above it. The top of the ranking is the commuter belt around Amsterdam, Utrecht and The Hague: small, high-income municipalities with driveways and a short drive to a large employer. The bottom is rural Friesland, Overijssel and the Bible belt, where cars are older, distances longer and incomes lower. The pattern is consistent with what the CBS itself reports, that electric cars first reach the households that can charge at home and buy new.

The big cities: many cars, more chargers

The four largest cities hold the largest fleets in absolute terms. Amsterdam has an estimated 39,838 private electric cars at a share of 17.8%, Rotterdam 33,980 at 15.7%, The Hague 32,512 at 18.2% and Utrecht 19,396 at 17.3%. Their shares sit close to the national average, yet their public charging density is far above it: The Hague offers 36.6 public charge points per 100 EVs and Rotterdam 32.2, against 15.1 nationally. That is the mirror image of the villa municipalities: where nobody has a driveway, the municipality has to put the charger on the street, and the Dutch cities have done so at scale.

Where public charging is thin, and where it is generous

Among the 230 municipalities with at least 2,000 electric cars, the number of public charge points per 100 EVs runs from 2.2 in Wijk bij Duurstede to 43.4 in Loon op Zand. A low figure is not automatically a problem: in Wijk bij Duurstede and Veendam most owners charge on their own driveway. It does matter for visitors. If you drive into one of the thin municipalities without a full battery, plan the charge before you arrive, or pick the P+R or garage with charge points from the charger map. In the generous municipalities the risk is the opposite one: plenty of posts, but each on a paid parking spot, so the parking tariff can exceed the electricity. The charger map prices both together.

The national fleet since 2019

The register counted 2,023,337 cars with electric drive on 1 January 2026, 21.5% of all 9,415,540 passenger cars, up 402,196 or 24.8% in a year. Diesel has fallen from 1,259,923 cars in 2019 to 582,363. The all-cars share is higher than the private-car share on the map because company and lease cars, 1,154,344 of them, electrify faster than private ones. The CBS definition of electric drive includes hybrids and plug-in hybrids alongside battery-only cars, so the battery-only fleet is smaller than the headline.

Methodology

Share per municipality: CBS custom table "Aandeel elektrische personenauto's per gemeente, 1 januari 2026", which counts passenger cars registered to natural persons that were insured during the previous year and excludes dealer stock, from the national vehicle register. Estimated EVs: that share applied to the CBS count of private passenger cars per municipality from the regional key figures (latest available year), rounded; treat it as an estimate within a few percent. Residents: CBS, 1 January 2026. Charge points: this site's extract of the national charge point register (NDW / DOT-NL), 78,584 locations with 196,063 charge points, each assigned to the municipality whose 2026 boundary contains it; private and semi-public posts are not included. Municipal boundaries: CBS via PDOK, generalised. Boundary changes between the CBS table and the register extract can shift a few dozen posts at municipal edges. Questions

Which Dutch municipality has the most electric cars?

By share of private cars, Rozendaal leads with 28.0% on 1 January 2026, followed by Laren (25.1%) and Amstelveen (24.8%). In absolute numbers Amsterdam has the most, with an estimated 39,838 electric cars registered to private owners.

How many electric cars are there in the Netherlands?

On 1 January 2026 the national vehicle register held 2,023,337 passenger cars with electric drive as main fuel, 21.5% of all 9,415,540 cars and including hybrids and plug-in hybrids under the CBS definition. That is 402,196 more than a year earlier, a rise of 24.8%.

Where is public charging most crowded?

Among municipalities with at least 2,000 electric cars, Wijk bij Duurstede has the fewest public charge points per 100 EVs (2.2) and Loon op Zand the most (43.4). The national figure is 15.1. Low numbers usually mean owners charge at home on a driveway; high numbers mean on-street parking with municipal charging programmes.

Where do these numbers come from?

The share per municipality is a CBS publication based on the national vehicle register, counting private cars insured in the previous year. Population and private-car counts come from CBS regional key figures. Charge points are this site's own extract of the national charge point register, assigned to municipalities by location. Where the charging still needs to go

Adoption and provision are different questions. We applied the EU’s own standard, 1.3 kW of public charging per electric car, to every two-kilometre hexagon in the country: the neighbourhood charging gap map.
