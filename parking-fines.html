Dutch parking fines in 2026: what you got, what it costs, what to do

There is no such thing as "a Dutch parking fine". There are three different letters, from three different offices, with different amounts, deadlines and ways to contest them. This page tells you which one is in your hand, what it will cost by city and by day of delay, how to pay it from abroad, and how to object with a letter you can copy. Every amount is the 2026 figure from the official source, listed at the bottom.

Last reviewed 30 September 2026. Amounts: Kostenbesluit naheffingsaanslag parkeerbelasting 2026 (Staatscourant 2025, 24417), Boetebase of the Public Prosecution Service 2026, CJIB surcharge table.

In one minute. Read the sender on the letter. A municipality or its tax office sent you a naheffingsaanslag parkeerbelasting: you did not pay for street parking, you owe €82.00 plus one hour, and you pay and object to the municipality. The CJIB in Leeuwarden sent you a beschikking: you parked where you may not, you owe €130 to €500 plus €9, you pay through cjib.nl and object to the public prosecutor. A company sent you a claim about a private car park: that is a contract dispute, not a fine. Six weeks to object in the first two cases. Everything below is the detail.

Step one: which letter did you get?

Dutch cities enforce parking with scan cars that photograph every plate on the street, wardens on foot and bicycle, and cameras at garage entrances and emission-zone borders. What they issue depends on what you did wrong, and the difference matters more than the amount.

Letter 1 · municipality

Naheffingsaanslag parkeerbelasting

You stood in a paid zone without paying, or for longer than you paid, or your app session covered the wrong zone or a mistyped plate. Street parking in Dutch cities is a municipal tax, so this is a tax assessment, not a penalty.

Amountup to €82.00 + 1 hour tariff
Senderthe municipality or its tax office
Object tothe municipality, within 6 weeks
Unpaidmunicipal bailiff, clamp or tow on sight
Abroadprivate collection agency, no EU route
Letter 2 · CJIB

Beschikking under the Mulder Act

You parked where parking is prohibited: a no-parking sign, the pavement, a loading bay, a disabled bay, a blue zone without a disc, in front of a driveway, on a cycle path. This is a traffic offence with a fixed national amount.

Letter 3 · a company

Private car park claim

You overstayed or did not pay in a privately run car park, supermarket lot or a garage with plate recognition. The operator or its collection agency writes to you. It is a contractual charge, not a fine, and none of the public enforcement powers apply.

Two other letters exist and both come on top of one of the three above: a wheel-clamp notice on the windscreen, and a towing notice or an empty parking space. Both are covered below.

Step two: what it costs in 2026

The municipal surcharge, city by city

The law caps the cost component at €82.00 for 2026, up from €78.80 in 2025 and €72.90 in 2024, indexed to consumer prices. To that the municipality adds one hour of parking at the tariff of the zone you stood in, which is why the same mistake costs more in Amsterdam than in Zwolle. Larger cities charge the full €82.00; a smaller town may charge less, and the exact figure is printed on your notice.

Step three: the clock is running. Here is exactly what happens

Municipal surcharge

  • Day 0The scan car or warden records the plate. The notice is printed on the spot and left on the car, or posted to the registered keeper. Postal notices to Dutch addresses arrive within about a week; to foreign addresses via a collection agency, weeks later.
  • Within the payment term, usually 4 to 6 weeksPay the municipality: online with the assessment number and plate, or by bank transfer using the payment reference on the letter. Nothing else is added.
  • Day 42Last day to object. The objection must be received, not sent, by then. Objecting does not automatically pause payment; write "ik verzoek om uitstel van betaling" in the letter and the municipality will normally grant deferral until it decides.
  • After the termA reminder (aanmaning) with a statutory fee, then a writ (dwangbevel) served by the municipal bailiff with higher costs, then seizure of goods or the vehicle. Cities with plate lists clamp or tow vehicles with several unpaid notices when they are next seen.

CJIB traffic fine

  • Day 0The warden or officer records the offence. You may find a note on the car, but the formal notice (beschikking) is posted by the CJIB to the registered keeper, typically within two to four weeks in the Netherlands.
  • Day 42Last day to appeal to the public prosecutor through the Digitaal Loket Verkeer on cjib.nl, using the CJIB number on the letter. Appealing suspends the payment term. English is accepted.
  • Day 56End of the eight-week payment term if you did not appeal. Pay on cjib.nl, by iDEAL from a Dutch account, or by bank transfer from abroad using the IBAN and reference on the letter.
  • First reminderThe fine plus 50 percent. €130 becomes €195 (plus the €9 already included).
  • Second reminderAnother 100 percent of the fine plus the amount of the first reminder. €130 has become €390.
  • EnforcementFor Dutch residents: bailiff, seizure, taking the car out of use, and as a last resort detention. For foreign residents: the case is transferred to the authority in your country under EU Framework Decision 2005/214, which enforces it as if it were a domestic fine.

How to pay, including from abroad

Municipal surcharge

  1. Find the assessment number (aanslagnummer) and the payment reference (betalingskenmerk) on the letter. Amsterdam, Rotterdam, The Hague and Utrecht all let you look up and pay a notice online by plate and assessment number on the municipality's tax pages; smaller towns give a bank account and reference.
  2. Pay by iDEAL if you have a Dutch bank, otherwise by SEPA transfer to the IBAN on the letter, quoting the payment reference exactly. A transfer from a German, Belgian or French account is a normal euro transfer and costs nothing extra.
  3. Keep the confirmation. If a reminder still arrives, reply with it; crossed payments are common when a transfer lands after the reminder run.

CJIB traffic fine

  1. Go to cjib.nl and choose Betalen. Enter the CJIB number and your plate or date of birth as printed on the notice.
  2. Dutch accounts pay by iDEAL. From abroad, use the bank transfer details on the letter: the IBAN of the CJIB and the CJIB number as reference, nothing else in the reference field.
  3. Cannot pay at once? Residents can request a payment plan for fines above €75 through the same site. Foreign residents should pay before the case is transferred; once it is with your home authority, its rules apply.

Private claim

Pay only after you have checked the sign. If the amount and the rule were clearly posted before you entered, the claim will stand and collection costs will exceed the original amount within weeks. If they were not, dispute it in writing and keep the photographs; an operator without clear signage has a weak case.

How to object, and what actually works

The Dutch system is strict but procedural. Objections succeed when they prove a fact; they fail when they explain an intention. Before writing, decide which of these you can prove:

  • You paid, for the right place and time, and the system missed it. Attach the app receipt or the bank line and the parking machine ticket. This is the most common successful objection.
  • You paid under a mistyped plate. One or two wrong characters, same zone, same time: most municipalities withdraw the surcharge on first objection when you attach the payment record and a photo of the plate.
  • You paid in the wrong zone code. Weaker, because you paid a different tariff, but a payment within minutes for an adjacent zone often gets the surcharge reduced to the difference or withdrawn. Attach the receipt and a map screenshot.
  • The machine was out of order and there was no working alternative. Photograph the error screen with the time visible. Note that most cities argue an app was available; this works best where the signs did not show a zone code.
  • The sign was missing, obscured or contradictory. Photograph the whole street view from where you parked, not just the sign. Municipal sign records can be requested.
  • You had a valid permit or a European disabled parking card in the windscreen. Send a copy and a photo of its position. Disabled card rules for paid zones differ by city, so quote the city's own rule.
  • The vehicle was not yours at the time. Sold, stolen, or a rental you had returned: send the transfer document, the police report or the rental return receipt.

What does not work: forgetting, being a few minutes over, not understanding Dutch signs, not knowing the zone was paid on a Sunday, being a tourist. The scan car has no grace period and the law does not require one.

Objecting to a municipal surcharge

  1. Write to the municipality named on the letter, within six weeks of its date. Most large cities have an online objection form on their tax pages; a letter or e-mail to the address on the notice also counts. Ask for confirmation of receipt.
  2. Include: your name and address, the assessment number, the plate, the date and place, what you object to, the evidence, a request for deferral of payment, and your signature.
  3. The municipality has to decide within six weeks of the objection deadline; it may extend once by six weeks. You receive a written decision.
  4. If it is rejected, you can appeal to the district court (rechtbank) within six weeks. The court fee for an individual is about €50 and is refunded if you win. Most people stop before this step, and for €90 that is a rational choice unless the principle matters.

Appealing a CJIB traffic fine

  1. Within six weeks of the date on the notice, open the Digitaal Loket Verkeer on cjib.nl and choose "in beroep gaan". Enter the CJIB number. You can write in English. Paper appeals go to the address printed on the notice; send them tracked.
  2. State the facts, attach photos and documents, and say what you want: the fine withdrawn or reduced. Do not pay first; appealing suspends the payment term.
  3. The public prosecutor decides in writing, normally within a few months. If the appeal is rejected the payment term restarts.
  4. If rejected, you can appeal to the cantonal judge (kantonrechter) within six weeks. For traffic fines you must first pay the amount as security (zekerheidstelling); it is refunded if you win. There is no separate court fee at this stage.

Foreign licence plates: the honest version

The single most repeated claim about Dutch parking fines is that they always follow a foreign plate home. The truth splits along the same line as everything else on this page.

A CJIB traffic fine does follow you

For fines above €70, the CJIB transfers unpaid cases to the authority of the country where you live under EU Framework Decision 2005/214, and that authority collects it under its own law. The CJIB sends close to a million notices a year to foreign addresses. For registration data the Netherlands uses the EU exchange for the eight offence types covered by the cross-border enforcement directive, and bilateral requests for other cases. Expect the letter at your home address within two to eight weeks, in your language or with a translation, and treat it as you would a fine from your own police.

A municipal surcharge does not have that route

Parking tax is not one of the offences the EU rules cover. A municipality cannot hand a surcharge to the CJIB for collection abroad, and it cannot use the EU registration exchange to find you. What it can do is hire a collection agency that requests the keeper's address from your national vehicle registry under that country's rules for private claims, then writes to you. In Germany, Belgium and Austria this generally works and the letter arrives within weeks. In other countries it works patchily or not at all: Amsterdam issued about 6,100 surcharges to French visitors in 2024 and 21 percent were paid.

Cities have noticed, and the response is physical. Since 1 July 2025 Amsterdam clamps a French-registered car as soon as it has two unpaid surcharges on record, and it keeps plate lists for repeat offenders of any nationality. If you ignored a notice on a previous trip, the risk is not a letter; it is finding a clamp on your return, with a 24-hour deadline before towing. There is no border check and no entry ban.

Rental and lease cars

The fastest route to your wallet. The rental company is the registered keeper, receives every notice, pays it and charges your card, adding an administration fee that most Dutch and international renters set between €25 and €60 per notice. You agreed to this in the rental contract. You can still object to the underlying notice yourself; ask the rental company for a copy and file within the six weeks.

Situations that trip people up

Paid by app, still fined

Three causes, in order of frequency: the wrong zone code (the sign nearest your car carries the code; a code from a street away is a different zone), a mistyped plate, and a session that never confirmed because the phone lost signal. All three produce a surcharge, and the first two are the most winnable objections on this page because your payment record proves intent and time. Screenshot the confirmation before you walk away, every time.

Blue zones

Free parking with a disc, a traffic rule rather than a tax. Set the disc to your arrival time rounded up to the next half hour, park no longer than the sign says, and move the car when the time is up; turning the disc without moving is an offence. No disc, wrong time or overstay is a €130 CJIB fine. Discs are free or a euro or two at ANWB shops, petrol stations and hardware stores; a physical disc is required, apps are not accepted in most municipalities.

Sundays and evenings

Many cities are free on Sunday and after 21:00; Amsterdam's centre is paid every hour of every day. The zone sign or the machine shows the paid hours. A surcharge outside paid hours is a clear objection: photograph the sign with the hours.

Disabled parking card

The blue European card is valid in the Netherlands on general disabled bays. Whether it also exempts you from paying in a paid zone depends on the municipality; some do, some limit it, some do not. Check the city's rule before you rely on it, and keep the card fully visible in the windscreen.

Charging bays

A bay marked for electric charging is for vehicles that are charging. Standing there without a cable connected is a Mulder offence at €130 where the sign is in place, and many cities tow from charging bays quickly. The charger map shows where the bays are and what the parking under them costs.

Resident permit areas

In Amsterdam most street spaces are in permit areas where visitors pay the visitor tariff, and a payment at the machine covers you; in some cities certain streets are permit-only and no payment makes you legal. The sign says "vergunninghouders". Where you see it without a tariff, do not park.

Clamped or towed: the next hour

  1. Clamped. The notice on the windscreen gives a phone number and the total: outstanding surcharges plus the release charge from the table above. Pay by card or online as instructed; a crew removes the clamp, usually within an hour or two. Miss the deadline on the notice (24 hours in most cities, three working days in Rotterdam) and the car is towed with the towing charge added.
  2. Towed in Amsterdam. Call 14 020. The car is at the municipal depot on Daniël Goedkoopstraat in Zuidoost, open 24 hours. Bring identification and the registration document or rental contract. Towing costs €452, storage is free for the first 24 hours and about €30 a day after that, paid by card. The parking notice that caused the tow is still owed.
  3. Towed elsewhere. The city's general number (14 plus the area code, for example 14 010 for Rotterdam) tells you where the car is and what it costs. Expect €200 to €450 plus storage.
  4. Objecting to a tow. Possible, in writing to the municipality, within six weeks, separately from the parking notice. It succeeds only if the car was legally parked or the tow was clearly disproportionate. Pay first, object after; the car is not released on the promise of an objection.

Not getting one in the first place

  • Use an app and stop the session when you leave; you pay to the minute and cannot overstay. Which app works where.
  • Read the zone code off the sign next to your car, not from memory. Photograph the sign.
  • Check paid hours before assuming a free Sunday or evening. City-by-city Sunday rules.
  • For a day in a city centre, use a P+R and public transport: €6 to €10 for the day against €25 to €90 for the same hours on the street. Where the P+R sites are.
  • If you park in a disabled bay, a loading bay or a charging bay for even a minute, expect a warden; these are the bays cities watch.
