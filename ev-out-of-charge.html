Your EV ran out of charge in the Netherlands

First make yourself safe. Then know this: in this country you are almost never far from a charger. We measured it, from 7,120 points across the land.

In one minute. Hazard lights on before you open the door. Get the car onto the hard shoulder, leave by the passenger side, and wait behind the barrier, not in the car. Call your breakdown service, or Rijkswaterstaat on 0800-8002 on a motorway, and give the number on the nearest hectometre post. Then relax a little: the nearest public charge point is a median 1.0 km from any point in the Netherlands, the nearest fast charger 3.5 km, and 96.7% of the country is within 10 km of a fast charger with no fault reported right now.

Make yourself safe first

  • Hazard lights on before you get out, not after.
  • Get as far onto the hard shoulder as you can, with the front wheels turned towards the barrier so the car cannot roll into the carriageway.
  • Leave through the passenger door. Opening the driver's door puts you in the lane.
  • Stand behind the crash barrier or well into the verge, away from the car. Being hit at 100 to 130 km/h is the real risk here, not the flat battery.
  • No warning triangle on a motorway. It is not compulsory to carry one in the Netherlands, and walking back down a hard shoulder to place it is more dangerous than not having it.
  • Call, and give the hectometre post number. Rijkswaterstaat is 0800-8002. Use 112 only if somebody is hurt or the car is stuck in a live lane.

What the breakdown service actually does

The ANWB says the Wegenwacht will put enough charge in the car to reach the nearest charge point or fast charger, rather than towing it to a garage. Whether a flat battery counts as a covered breakdown or is billed on top depends on your policy. That is worth checking before you need it, because it is one of the few exclusions people only discover at the roadside.

Most cars keep a reserve past zero, usually another 10 to 15 km depending on model, battery age and temperature. Treat that as enough to reach an exit, not as range to plan with.

Does a broken charger strand you?

Almost never, and this surprised us. If the nearest fast charger is completely out of order, driving on to the next working one adds 0.09 km on average. Only 0.2% of the country would have to travel more than 5 km further, and the worst case anywhere is 7.0 km. Dutch charging density is high enough that a fault is an annoyance rather than an emergency.

Common questions

How far am I likely to be from a charger if I run flat in the Netherlands?

From a random point on Dutch land the nearest public charge point of any kind is a median 1.0 km away, and the nearest fast charger 3.5 km. 96.7% of the country is within 10 km of a fast charger that is working right now. Measured on 2026-10-19 10:04 UTC over 7,120 points on a 2 km grid.

Does it matter if the nearest fast charger is broken?

Almost never. Skipping to the next working one adds 0.09 km on average, and only 0.2% of the country would have to travel more than 5 km further. 172 of 1562 fast chargers, 11.0%, are reporting every connector out of order at the moment.

What should I do first if I stop on a motorway?

Hazard lights on before you get out. Get the car as far onto the hard shoulder as you can, with the front wheels turned towards the barrier. Leave through the passenger door, not the driver's door, and get behind the crash barrier, away from the car. Do not sit in the car and do not put a warning triangle out on a motorway. Then call for help and give the number on the nearest hectometre post.

Who do I call in the Netherlands?

Your own breakdown service, or Rijkswaterstaat on 0800-8002 for a motorway. Call 112 only if someone is hurt or the car is in a live lane.

Will the breakdown service charge my car or tow it?

The ANWB says the Wegenwacht gives the car enough charge to reach the nearest charge point or fast charger. Whether a flat battery counts as a covered breakdown, or is billed as an extra, depends on your policy, so check yours before you need it rather than after.

How far can I actually drive after the car says zero?

Most cars keep a reserve and will do roughly another 10 to 15 km, depending on the model, the age of the battery and the temperature. Treat it as enough to reach an exit, not as range you can plan with.
