How to pay for parking in Belgium (the 4411 question)

By far the most-searched question from visitors is simply "how do I pay for parking in Brussels / Belgium?" The answer across almost every city is the same service: 4411. Use the app if you have a foreign phone; use SMS only if you have a Belgian mobile number.

Option 1: the 4411 app (best for tourists and expats)

  1. Download 4411 and add your number plate and a bank card. It works with any phone number, including a foreign one.
  2. Park, then open the app. If you share location it suggests the right zone; otherwise read the zone code printed on the side of the meter (coloured by zone: red, green, grey, event).
  3. Start the session and pick your plate. You only pay for the time you actually use, and stop the session when you leave. Transaction fee is about €0.25 per session on top of the street tariff.

Option 2: pay by SMS (needs a Belgian SIM)

Text the zone code, a space, then your full number plate to 4411 - for example GE1234 1ABC123. To stop, text Q to 4411. SMS parking only works with a Belgian mobile number (Proximus, Telenet, Base, Scarlet, Orange, KPN), and the transaction fee is about €0.30 per session.

Other ways to pay

  • Street meter: pay by card or coins and display the ticket. Fine as a backup, but you pay for a fixed block whether you use it or not.
  • Seety: a popular Belgian app that helps you find free and cheap parking and pay, and shows the rules per street.
  • City / operator apps: parking.brussels in Brussels, plus EasyPark and Yellowbrick, which many expats already have from the Netherlands.

Expat tip: no Belgian SIM? Skip SMS entirely. Install the 4411 app (or Seety / EasyPark), add your plate once, and you can pay in any 4411 city without a local number.

Belgian parking "fines": the retributie explained

A Belgian parking penalty is usually a retributie (Dutch) or redevance (French): a civil charge, not a criminal fine. It is a flat amount, often the maximum daily tariff, commonly about €25 and up to roughly €47 in busy sectors. You get it for not paying, an expired session, or the wrong zone code.

Because it is a civil charge run by the city or a private operator (Indigo, Q-Park, OPC, City Parking and others), it is not handled by the police. You pay it on the notice or the operator's website. If you ignore it, it escalates to debt collection and, eventually, a bailiff.

Does it follow a foreign plate home? Increasingly, yes

This is the myth worth killing. For years, foreign-plated cars mostly got away with it: Brussels recovered under 10% of charges billed to foreign plates in 2023-2024. That era is closing:

  • Since November 2025, Parking.brussels uses a specialist international debt-recovery firm. In the first quarter of 2026 alone it collected abroad as much as in the whole of 2025.
  • Ghent transfers unpaid foreign-plate charges to a foreign collection partner.
  • Rental cars are the fastest route to you: the rental company pays the charge and bills your card, usually with an admin fee added.

If you drive a Dutch, German, French or other EU plate, treat a Belgian retributie as a real bill that will reach you. See our full guide to Dutch parking fines and cross-border enforcement for how the EU system works next door.

The expensive mistake

The low emission zone (LEZ) foreign-plate trap

This catches thousands of foreign drivers who never even parked wrong. Brussels, Antwerp and Ghent all run camera-enforced low emission zones. A foreign car that is not registered is fined even when it meets the emission standard, because the camera only sees an unknown plate.

Each city is separate: an Antwerp registration does not cover Brussels or Ghent. Register on the official sites only - lez.brussels, slimnaarantwerpen.be, stad.gent. Ignore any site selling a "Belgian city sticker": there is no such windscreen sticker, and those sites are scams. Dutch-registered cars are exempt from registering because Belgium can read Dutch plate data directly.

Blue zones and the parking disc

Outside the paid centres, much of Belgium is blue zone (blauwe zone / zone bleue): parking is free, but you must display a cardboard parking disc set to your arrival time, rounded up to the next half hour. The maximum stay (usually 1 or 2 hours) is on the sign. No disc, or an overstay, means a charge of around €25 or more. Discs are free from newsagents, petrol stations, bookshops and many supermarkets.

Set your disc correctly:

→
14:30

Rule: if you arrive exactly on a half hour, use that time; otherwise round up. Arrive 14:00 → set 14:00. Arrive 14:01 → set 14:30. Arrive 14:31 → set 15:00. A digital disc app is not accepted in most places, so keep a physical disc in the car.

When is parking free, and the cheapest way in

Free times

In most Belgian cities on-street paid parking runs Monday to Saturday, roughly 09:00 to 18:00 (later in some Brussels communes). That means Sundays, public holidays and evenings are usually free on-street, except a few red zones. It is one of the most-searched questions ("is parking free on Sunday in Brussels?") and the answer is generally yes - but the sign is the final word.

Park and Ride (P+R): park cheap on the edge, ride in

The city centre is the most expensive place to park. A P+R lets you leave the car cheaply or free on the ring and take a tram, bus or shared bike into town:

  • Brussels: 7 P+R sites. P+R Stalle is free for everyone; Ceria and Crainhem are free with a STIB season ticket, others cheap with a metro journey of 3+ stops.
  • Ghent: large edge P+R with frequent trams; live P+R and garage counts are on the map above.
  • Antwerp: P+R Linkeroever, Luchtbal and others with tram/bus into the centre - and they sit outside the strict LEZ core.
Get around

Parked the car? Grab a bike, e-bike or scooter

Belgian cities are compact and bike-first. Once you have parked at a P+R or garage, shared micromobility is usually the fastest and cheapest way into the centre. Live Ghent e-bikes are on the map above.

🚆

Blue-bike

Shared bikes at about 100 train stations nationwide - ideal if you arrive by P+R next to a station. Unlock with the app or an MOBIB card; roughly €1 to €3 per trip on a small yearly membership.

🛴

Dott · Bolt · Lime · Pony

Dockless e-scooters and e-bikes in Brussels, Antwerp and Ghent. Scan to unlock, ride, then park in an allowed spot. Pay per minute in the app. Great for the last mile from a P+R.

🚲

Villo! & Donkey Republic

Villo! is Brussels' docked bike-share with hundreds of stations; short trips are cheap or free on a day pass. Donkey Republic serves Bruges, Ghent and more with app-unlock bikes.

Park once, then go car-free

The winning move in almost every Belgian city: park at a cheap Park and Ride on the edge, skip the low emission zone and the centre tariffs entirely, and take a shared bike, scooter or tram the rest of the way.

Where this data comes from

The live map and garage counts on this page are pulled directly from official open government data, in real time, with no middleman:

  • City of Ghent Open Data (data.stad.gent): real-time car-park and Park and Ride occupancy, and live Dott and Bolt shared-bike locations, updated every minute.
  • parking.brussels and the Brussels Mobility open datastore: tariffs, zones, P+R and the region's parking observatory.
  • lez.brussels, slimnaarantwerpen.be, stad.gent: official low emission zone rules and foreign-plate registration.

We show live data for Ghent first because it publishes the most complete open, real-time feed. As other Belgian cities open up equivalent feeds, we will add them here. Nothing on this page requires an account or an API key.
